03🔬 Core 1–2 notesMore than labels

More than labels.

Reproductive systems / Core

Organs are a route, not a list.

Male reproductive system: sperm are made in the testes, kept at an optimum cooler temperature in the scrotum, moved through the sperm ducts and mixed with nutritive prostate fluid to form semen. The urethra carries semen through the penis to the outside.

Female reproductive system: mature egg cells are released from the ovaries into the oviducts. Fertilisation takes place in an oviduct before the early embryo moves to the uterus, where it can implant.

Testesproduce sperm + testosterone
Oviductciliated tube + site of fertilisation
Uterusembryo implants + fetus develops
Cervixmuscular neck holding the fetus in place
Vaginareceives penis + birth canal

Core vocabulary / say it preciselydefinitions to learn
TermExam-ready definition
GameteA haploid sex cell: sperm in males or egg cell in females.
FertilisationThe fusion of the nuclei from a male gamete and a female gamete to form a diploid zygote.
ZygoteThe single diploid cell formed at fertilisation.
EmbryoA ball of cells formed when the zygote divides repeatedly by mitosis.
ImplantationThe embryo attaching to and entering the lining of the uterus.

07Extended 9–10Exchange & protection

The placenta is
selective.

Placental exchange: useful molecules cross between maternal and fetal blood. Some pathogens and toxins can cross too.

→Mother → fetus

glucose, amino acids, oxygen, vitamins and mineral ions

←Fetus → mother

carbon dioxide and urea for removal by the mother

Why no direct mixing?Pressure and blood-group differences could damage fetal capillaries. Exchange happens across thin membranes.
Extended 10 / risk check

Not every molecule should cross.

Some pathogens and toxins pass across the placenta and affect the fetus.
